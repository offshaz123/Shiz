# Executive Tints & Repairs — the numbers

What it costs to run the place, what a car is worth, and how many cars a month
the target actually requires. Built 29 September 2026 from figures given by
the owner. **Everything here is a model, not a measurement** — the actual
trading figures are still to come.

Trading since roughly **5-6 September 2026**.

## What a car actually costs to do

A 30-metre roll is about £100 and does seven to nine full cars.

| | |
|---|---|
| Film per full car | ~£12.50 |
| Consumables | ~£3.00 |
| **Material cost per car** | **~£15.50** |

That is a **90%+ gross margin**. This business is not constrained by
materials. It is constrained by how many cars come through the door and what
each one pays.

**Break-even is 23-31 cars a month.** Below that the business pays to work.

### The finding that matters most

Revenue barely moves across that table — every route to £5,000 net passes
through roughly **£10,000 a month**. What changes enormously is the *number of
cars* needed to get there:

- At a **£150** average: **68 cars**
- At a **£200** average: **50 cars**

**Eighteen fewer cars a month for identical profit.** That is four working days
of the fitter's time, freed by pricing rather than by selling harder.

Average job value is therefore the single biggest lever in the business, ahead
of ad spend, ahead of conversion rate, ahead of everything else.

## Capacity ceiling

50 cars a month is **2.3 cars a day** on a five-day week. Whether that is
comfortable or impossible depends on how long a full car takes — still to be
confirmed. If a full car is 3 hours, one fitter tops out somewhere around
50-60 cars a month and growth past that needs a second pair of hands, not more
advertising.

## Where the cars have to come from

Google Ads produced roughly **15 real phone conversations a month** in late
September.

| Close rate | Cars/month from Google | Ad cost per customer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 in 3 | 5 | £157 |
| 1 in 2 | 8 | £104 |
| 2 in 3 | 10 | £79 |

**Google Ads supplies about 5 of the 50 cars needed.** Tripled, it supplies 15.
It cannot reach the target alone and was never going to. The remainder has to
come from the map pack, referrals, repeat work and walk-ins — channels that
cost little or nothing and are currently doing almost none of the work.

## The VAT cliff

£10,000 a month is **£120,000 a year**. The UK VAT registration threshold is
around **£90,000** — confirm the current figure with an accountant.

Crossing it means VAT on sales: roughly £20,000 a year unless it is built into
prices, which would remove about a third of the planned profit.

**This needs an accountant's advice at £7,500 a month, not at £10,000.** It
changes pricing, and it is far cheaper to plan for than to discover.

## Decisions

**29 Sep — Meta Ads switched off.** The owner's reasoning, which the structure
of the two channels supports: Google Search reaches someone who has decided
they want tints and is looking for someone to do it, so a call converts to a
booking the same day. Meta interrupts someone who was not thinking about it,
which costs more per lead and converts worse for a service booked by
appointment. £200/month released.

## Still needed

The model above is built entirely on costs. These are the missing halves:

1. **Cars completed** since 5-6 September, all sources.
2. **Actual takings** over the same period.
3. **Source of each customer** — Google, walk-in, referral, map listing.
4. **Real average job value** — what people pay, not the price list.
5. **Hours per full car** — this sets the capacity ceiling.
6. **Is the fitter busy or idle?** The most important question here. Idle
   capacity means marketing is the constraint. A full diary means more
   advertising only builds a queue.
7. **Owner's drawings**, and the missing overheads listed above.

Questions 1, 5 and 6 together decide whether the next pound goes into
advertising or into fixing the phone.
