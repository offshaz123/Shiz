# Bubble tea — research, 26 October 2026

Research for a possible bubble tea shop, commissioned after the tinting
business was judged not to be paying. Everything below is desk research from
public sources; figures from suppliers and franchise directories are marketing
material and are flagged where they matter.

## The headline findings

1. **The sugar-percentage idea is not a USP.** Adjustable sugar and ice is
   standard across the whole category — T4, Chatime, Gong Cha, CoCo, Mooboo all
   offer it. Building a shop on it means opening with no differentiator at all.
2. **T4 already trades in Walthamstow**, at 2 Palace Parade, E17 7BU. So does
   BoBAR, at 460 Hoe Street. The home patch is taken.
3. **Every East London town centre with real footfall already has 2-5 boba
   shops.** The "busy street with no boba" does not appear to exist locally.
4. **Winter demand is a genuine unknown.** No hard UK seasonality data exists
   publicly, and one supplier states plainly that hot bubble tea is "not yet a
   mainstream, proven item in the UK market".
5. **Yes, it can earn more than tinting** — roughly 3-4x at volume — but
   break-even is 49-72 cups a day against established competition.

### T4, the chain the owner cited

Branches at Canary Wharf (16 Crossrail Walk, E14 5HX), Greenwich (24 Greenwich
Church Street, SE10 9BJ), Farringdon (90 Cowcross Street, EC1M 6BH), plus
Walthamstow, Angel, Strand, Westfield, Hayes, Seven Sisters, Peckham and
Bristol. Prices run roughly £3.60 (Winter Melon) to £6.00 (Brown Sugar Pearl
Milk Tea), averaging about £4.77 on aggregator listings; delivery-app prices run
higher than in-store.

## What the competition is actually bad at

The consistent complaint across reviews of local incumbents is **service, not
product**: BoBAR Walthamstow draws comments about slow service at busy times,
Mooboo Romford about staff attitude, Gong Cha Romford sits at 3/5 on
Tripadvisor. In a category where the drinks are near-identical, this is the
most defensible gap available.

## Candidate USPs, ranked

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Sugar % customisation | **Dead.** Universal in the category |
| Cheaper than the chains | **Dead.** They buy better than you ever will |
| **Speed and service** | **Strongest.** The incumbents' weakest point, costs nothing but management |
| **Fully halal, clearly labelled** | **Strong locally.** Large Muslim population in Waltham Forest and Redbridge; gelatin in toppings is a real barrier |
| **Late opening** | **Strong.** Most close 21:00-22:00; a midnight close near a cinema or shisha strip owns the evening |
| Desserts alongside drinks | **Necessary, not differentiating.** It is the winter hedge, and Gotham Boba in Romford already runs this model |
| Fresh fruit / low sugar positioning | Possible, harder to execute, higher waste |

## Winter

No published UK sales splits by season. What is known:

- Suppliers push hot milk teas, and winter flavours (peppermint, chocolate,
  gingerbread, spiced apple, caramel, roasted tea) for the cold months.
- The same suppliers caution that hot bubble tea with toppings is unproven in
  the UK.
- December is cited as a strong month for festive flavours.
- Mooboo has run hot drinks at £4.50 in off-peak weekday windows — a
  time-limited promotion, not evidence of year-round demand.

**Treat a significant winter dip as the base case.** The honest way to size it
is to ask a trading operator, or to trade a market stall through a winter
before signing a lease. Desserts, hot drinks and coffee are the hedge.

## Market

UK market estimates range from $168M to $210M with growth forecast between
5.1% and 13.9% a year, depending on whose report you read. All are
vendor-driven. No reliable count of UK bubble tea shops exists; Gong Cha alone
is reported at 225+ UK stores.

Competition is also shifting: mainstream cafés and chains are adding bubble tea
to existing menus, which erodes the standalone shop's position.

## Marketing, if it goes ahead

Boba is not a Google Search business — nobody searches then drives five miles.
It is won on:

1. **TikTok and Instagram.** The category exists because of them. This is the
   one place Meta/TikTok spend makes sense for this owner, having failed badly
   for tinting (£24.86 a click) — the product is visual and impulse-bought by
   exactly that audience.
2. **Google Business Profile.** "Bubble tea near me" is searched heavily and
   the map pack decides it. Free.
3. **Delivery apps.** Significant volume for boba, but 25-35% commission eats
   most of the margin. Use for discovery, drive repeat custom in-store.
4. **Loyalty card from day one.** Students buy in groups and return weekly.
5. **Schools, sixth forms and colleges** within walking distance — the core
   customer.

## Recommendation

The category can out-earn tinting by 3-4x at volume. But on this research the
local market is saturated, the intended USP is not one, and winter is
unquantified.

**Do not sign a lease on desk research.** Test the product and the demand on a
market stall or event pitch for £3,000-5,000 first — through a winter. That
tests the drinks, the hours, the owner's appetite for it, and the seasonality
question, for 3% of the cost of being wrong in a shop with a personal guarantee
on a 10-year lease.
